# Validation report — pystatistics `survival` v4.2.0

> This report validates **pystatistics survival v4.2.0**, installed from **pypi**. Evidence lives in `artifacts/survival/v4.2.0/`. It was generated by `render_report.py` from frozen artifacts — **do not edit numbers by hand**; fix the artifact or the renderer and re-render.

**Subsystem:** Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, Cox PH, discrete-time) (`pystatistics.survival`)

## 1. What statistical procedure is implemented?

Time-to-event analysis under one module. kaplan_meier estimates the product-limit survival curve S(t) with Greenwood standard errors and transformed confidence bands. survdiff is the log-rank (and G-rho weighted) test comparing survival across groups, reporting a chi-square statistic, degrees of freedom, observed/expected events per group, and a p-value. coxph fits the Cox proportional-hazards model by Newton-Raphson on the partial likelihood, reporting log hazard ratios, hazard ratios (exp coef), standard errors from the observed information, asymptotic-normal Wald z-statistics, p-values, Harrell's concordance, and the null/model partial log-likelihood. discrete_time fits a discrete-time survival model by expanding the data to person-period (long) format and fitting a logistic regression, reporting covariate hazard ratios and a per-interval baseline hazard.

## 2. Which algorithms are used?

- Kaplan-Meier (kaplan_meier), CPU: the product-limit estimator S(t) = prod(1 - d_j/n_j) over event times, with the Greenwood variance Var(S(t)) = S(t)^2 * sum(d_j / (n_j (n_j - d_j))). Confidence bands via the log (R default), plain, or log-log transformation. fp64, deterministic; a single sorted pass over the data.
- Log-rank / G-rho (survdiff), CPU: at each distinct event time, observed minus expected events per group are accumulated with the Mantel-Haenszel hypergeometric variance; the test statistic is the quadratic form (O-E)' V^-1 (O-E) on the first K-1 groups, referred to chi-square with K-1 df. rho > 0 gives the Fleming-Harrington weighted family (rho=1 is Peto-Peto / Gehan-Wilcoxon). fp64, deterministic.
- Cox proportional hazards (coxph), CPU only: Newton-Raphson on the partial log-likelihood with Efron's (R default) or Breslow's handling of tied event times. Each iteration computes the score vector and observed information by sweeping the risk sets; standard errors are sqrt of the diagonal of the inverted information at the solution; Wald z = coef/se referred to the normal. Step-halving caps the Newton step to prevent exp-overflow. Harrell's concordance is computed from all comparable pairs. fp64, deterministic.
- Discrete-time survival (discrete_time): the data are expanded to person-period format (one row per subject per interval at risk), then a binomial GLM is fit with interval indicators (the baseline hazard) plus covariates. The logistic fit is delegated to pystatistics.regression.fit(family='binomial'), so it inherits regression's CPU and GPU backends; covariate hazard ratios are exp(coef) and the per-interval baseline hazard is expit(interval coef).
- Backends: kaplan_meier, survdiff, and coxph are CPU-only and expose no backend= parameter (see the GPU reconciliation below). Only discrete_time takes backend= ('cpu' default, 'gpu', 'gpu_fp64' [CUDA-only exact path], or 'auto'), which it forwards to the regression binomial GLM that does the actual fitting.

## 3. How does it compare to R survival::survfit / survdiff / coxph?

The R survival package (Therneau) is the gold-standard reference for time-to-event analysis. The head-to-head pulls survfit() for the Kaplan-Meier curve, survdiff() for the log-rank test, and coxph() for the proportional-hazards fit, all on the canonical survival::lung dataset (NCCTG advanced lung cancer; Loprinzi et al. 1994). Discrete-time survival has no single R reference function, so it is validated against R's glm(family=binomial) fit on the identical person-period design that pystatistics constructs internally (the design is reconstructed in the driver and guarded: the reconstructed row count must equal the library's person_period_n). Agreement in the survival curve, the test statistic, the coefficient/HR/SE/z/p vectors, the concordance, and the partial log-likelihood is the correctness claim.

## 4. What numerical tolerances are expected?

- **km_vs_r:** Kaplan-Meier matches R survfit() to fp64 round-off: event times and at-risk counts are bit-identical, and survival, Greenwood standard error, and the log-transformed confidence bands agree to ~1e-15 relative. The comparison is restricted to event times (where R's summary reports rows), the same set pystatistics emits.
- **logrank_vs_r:** The log-rank chi-square statistic and its p-value match R survdiff() to ~1e-14; observed events are integer-identical and expected events agree to ~1e-14. (Default rho=0 standard log-rank; the G-rho family is implemented but not part of the lung head-to-head.)
- **coxph_vs_r:** Cox PH coefficients, hazard ratios, standard errors, Wald z-statistics, p-values, and the model partial log-likelihood match R coxph() (ties='efron') to fp64 round-off (~1e-13 to 1e-15 relative) on the lung age+sex+ph.ecog model.
- **coxph_concordance:** Harrell's concordance agrees with R only to ~1e-4, NOT to round-off. This is a definitional difference, not a defect: pystatistics computes the classic Harrell C from comparable pairs, while R's survival >= 3.x reports a tie-aware concordance (Therneau's concordancefit, which counts time ties and is computed via an O(n log n) balanced-tree algorithm). The two definitions differ at the fourth decimal on data with tied times; both are valid C-statistics.
- **ties_efron_vs_breslow:** Efron (the default, matching R) and Breslow are both implemented for tied event times. The validation pins ties='efron' on both sides; Efron is the more accurate approximation and R's default. Breslow agreement is not separately tabulated here.
- **discrete_vs_r:** Discrete-time covariate coefficients, standard errors, Wald z-statistics, and p-values match R glm(binomial) on the identical person-period design to fp64 round-off (~1e-14). The model is validated on coarse quantile-binned intervals (see limitations) so the logistic baseline is identified on both sides.

## Constitutional compliance & GPU backend reconciliation

This validation tracks the survival module against the PyStatistics Constitution (CONVENTIONS.md). The 4.0.0 audit surfaced six findings (C1-C3 API-shape, O1-O2 Cox performance, and a GPU convergence issue uncovered while probing discrete-time on GPU). All of the actionable ones were fixed across 4.1.0 and 4.2.0; the status of each is recorded below. The numerical results are unaffected by any of these changes.

**GPU backend reconciliation.** The constitution lists survival.coxph as a deliberate NO-GPU module (the partial likelihood is inherently sequential). The public API honors this: kaplan_meier, survdiff, and coxph expose no backend= parameter; KM and log-rank are cheap one-pass reductions and Cox PH is sequential (and, as of 4.2.0, O(n log n) on the CPU). The one GPU-capable path is discrete_time, which forwards backend= to pystatistics.regression.fit(family='binomial') over the person-period expansion (a genuinely large logistic regression). In 4.0.0 that GPU path was unreliable: it intermittently FAILED LOUD on Apple Silicon (MPS) at moderate n. 4.2.0 traced this to a GPU GLM convergence GATE that rejected correct float32 fits for not meeting an unreachable float64 tolerance (NOT ill-conditioning, as the old error claimed). With that fixed, discrete_time on GPU is now reliable and verified on MPS and CUDA; 4.2.0 also exposes backend='gpu_fp64' (the exact double-precision CUDA path). The constitution gained amendment A6 ('PyStatistics does not help'): an explicit request is honored exactly or fails loud — never a silent CPU/precision fallback, and never a false refusal of a correct lower-precision fit.

**Findings:**

- **C1 — Coefficient solutions lacked the uniform .conf_int accessor** _(RESOLVED in 4.1.0)_: 4.1.0 added a library-wide .conf_int (Wald) + conf_level= across the coefficient models, including CoxSolution; it matches R's summary.coxph hazard-ratio intervals to ~1e-15 and removed the hardcoded 1.96 from the Cox summary. (DiscreteTimeSolution still lacks .conf_int — a minor remaining gap, tracked separately.)
- **C2 — KMSolution used .se / .ci_lower / .ci_upper rather than the uniform names** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 added KMSolution.standard_errors (Greenwood SE) and KMSolution.conf_int (the (m,2) [lower,upper] band), matching the library-wide accessor names. The legacy .se/.ci_lower/.ci_upper remain (additive).
- **C3 — Stratified KM/Cox raised the builtin NotImplementedError** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 added NotImplementedFeatureError (subclasses both PyStatisticsError and the builtin NotImplementedError); stratified KM/Cox now raise it, routing every survival error through core.exceptions while staying catchable as NotImplementedError.
- **O1 — Harrell's concordance was an O(n^2) nested Python loop** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 replaced the all-pairs double loop with an O(n log n) Fenwick-tree count, reproducing the existing Harrell definition exactly. See the scaling study: the pystatistics/R Cox wall ratio went from growing 23x->812x to flat ~1-2.6x.
- **O2 — Cox partial-likelihood/score/information recomputed risk sets per event time** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 replaced the per-event-time risk-mask recomputation with vectorized reverse-cumulative-sum risk sets (S0/S1/S2) indexed via searchsorted — one O(n) sweep per Newton iteration. Together with O1 this makes Cox PH O(n log n), matching R; a full fit at n=8000 dropped from ~10s to ~31ms. Results bit-for-bit unchanged.
- **G1 — GPU GLM falsely rejected correct float32 fits (discrete-time on GPU)** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: Probing discrete_time on GPU exposed a GPU GLM convergence gate that held the strict float64 tolerance on the float32 path: a correct, well-conditioned float32 fit that reached the float32 floor was declared non-converged and failed loud (most often on MPS). The fit was correct (matched CPU to the float32 tier). 4.2.0 decides float32 acceptance by the relative Newton decrement (a stationarity test) instead; genuinely-unreliable fits still fail loud, with no silent CPU/precision fallback. Verified on MPS and CUDA across binomial/Poisson/Gamma.

## 6. What are the known limitations?

- Cox PH is CPU-only by construction — the partial likelihood is inherently sequential and is not a GPU target (a constitutional decision, not a gap). As of 4.2.0 the CPU implementation is O(n log n) (vectorized reverse-cumsum risk sets + a Fenwick-tree concordance), matching R's complexity; the scaling study shows a flat pystatistics/R wall ratio. True O(n) is impossible — the event-time sort is O(n log n), and R is O(n log n) too.
- Harrell's concordance differs from R's tie-aware concordance at ~1e-4 (a definitional difference; see tolerances). Both are valid C-statistics.
- Stratified Kaplan-Meier and stratified Cox PH are not implemented (strata=... raises NotImplementedFeatureError). The validation covers the unstratified models that R's lung examples use.
- Discrete-time survival is validated on coarse quantile-binned intervals (5 bins), not the intervals=None default. On continuous data the default uses every unique event time as its own interval, producing hundreds of single-event intervals that separate perfectly — discrete-time methods are designed for genuinely binned time, so the coarse-interval head-to-head is the meaningful comparison.
- discrete_time on GPU (backend='gpu'/'gpu_fp64') is reliable as of 4.2.0 (the GPU GLM convergence gate was fixed). It is correctness-validated here on CPU against R; the GPU paths inherit the regression binomial GPU backend, validated separately in the regression report.
- Only the Efron tie handling (R's default) is tabulated in the head-to-head; Breslow is implemented but not separately validated here.
- Tiny real datasets (lung, n~227) sit near R's timer resolution for KM and log-rank; the synthetic scaling study, not the lung timings, carries the rigorous performance claim.
