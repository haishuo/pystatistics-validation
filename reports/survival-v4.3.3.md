# Validation report — pystatistics `survival` v4.3.3

> This report validates **pystatistics survival v4.3.3**, installed from **pypi**. Evidence lives in `artifacts/survival/v4.3.3/`. It was generated by `render_report.py` from frozen artifacts — **do not edit numbers by hand**; fix the artifact or the renderer and re-render.

**Subsystem:** Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, Cox PH, discrete-time) (`pystatistics.survival`)

## 1. What statistical procedure is implemented?

Time-to-event analysis under one module. kaplan_meier estimates the product-limit survival curve S(t) with Greenwood standard errors and transformed confidence bands. survdiff is the log-rank (and G-rho weighted) test comparing survival across groups, reporting a chi-square statistic, degrees of freedom, observed/expected events per group, and a p-value. coxph fits the Cox proportional-hazards model by Newton-Raphson on the partial likelihood, reporting log hazard ratios, hazard ratios (exp coef), standard errors from the observed information, asymptotic-normal Wald z-statistics, p-values, Harrell's concordance, and the null/model partial log-likelihood. discrete_time fits a discrete-time survival model by expanding the data to person-period (long) format and fitting a logistic regression, reporting covariate hazard ratios and a per-interval baseline hazard.

## 2. Which algorithms are used?

- Kaplan-Meier (kaplan_meier), CPU: the product-limit estimator S(t) = prod(1 - d_j/n_j) over event times, with the Greenwood variance Var(S(t)) = S(t)^2 * sum(d_j / (n_j (n_j - d_j))). Confidence bands via the log (R default), plain, or log-log transformation. fp64, deterministic; a single sorted pass over the data.
- Log-rank / G-rho (survdiff), CPU: at each distinct event time, observed minus expected events per group are accumulated with the Mantel-Haenszel hypergeometric variance; the test statistic is the quadratic form (O-E)' V^-1 (O-E) on the first K-1 groups, referred to chi-square with K-1 df. rho > 0 gives the Fleming-Harrington weighted family (rho=1 is Peto-Peto / Gehan-Wilcoxon). fp64, deterministic.
- Cox proportional hazards (coxph), CPU only: Newton-Raphson on the partial log-likelihood with Efron's (R default) or Breslow's handling of tied event times. Each iteration computes the score vector and observed information by sweeping the risk sets; standard errors are sqrt of the diagonal of the inverted information at the solution; Wald z = coef/se referred to the normal. Step-halving caps the Newton step to prevent exp-overflow. Harrell's concordance is computed from all comparable pairs. fp64, deterministic.
- Discrete-time survival (discrete_time): the data are expanded to person-period format (one row per subject per interval at risk), then a binomial GLM is fit with interval indicators (the baseline hazard) plus covariates. The logistic fit is delegated to pystatistics.regression.fit(family='binomial'), so it inherits regression's CPU and GPU backends; covariate hazard ratios are exp(coef) and the per-interval baseline hazard is expit(interval coef).
- Backends: kaplan_meier, survdiff, and coxph are CPU-only and expose no backend= parameter (see the GPU reconciliation below). Only discrete_time takes backend= ('cpu' default, 'gpu', 'gpu_fp64' [CUDA-only exact path], or 'auto'), which it forwards to the regression binomial GLM that does the actual fitting.

## 3. How does it compare to R survival::survfit / survdiff / coxph?

The R survival package (Therneau) is the gold-standard reference for time-to-event analysis. The head-to-head pulls survfit() for the Kaplan-Meier curve, survdiff() for the log-rank test, and coxph() for the proportional-hazards fit, all on the canonical survival::lung dataset (NCCTG advanced lung cancer; Loprinzi et al. 1994). Discrete-time survival has no single R reference function, so it is validated against R's glm(family=binomial) fit on the identical person-period design that pystatistics constructs internally (the design is reconstructed in the driver and guarded: the reconstructed row count must equal the library's person_period_n). Agreement in the survival curve, the test statistic, the coefficient/HR/SE/z/p vectors, the concordance, and the partial log-likelihood is the correctness claim.

## 4. What numerical tolerances are expected?

- **km_vs_r:** Kaplan-Meier matches R survfit() to fp64 round-off: event times and at-risk counts are bit-identical, and survival, Greenwood standard error, and the log-transformed confidence bands agree to ~1e-15 relative. The comparison is restricted to event times (where R's summary reports rows), the same set pystatistics emits.
- **logrank_vs_r:** The log-rank chi-square statistic and its p-value match R survdiff() to ~1e-14; observed events are integer-identical and expected events agree to ~1e-14. (Default rho=0 standard log-rank; the G-rho family is implemented but not part of the lung head-to-head.)
- **coxph_vs_r:** Cox PH coefficients, hazard ratios, standard errors, Wald z-statistics, p-values, and the model partial log-likelihood match R coxph() (ties='efron') to fp64 round-off (~1e-13 to 1e-15 relative) on the lung age+sex+ph.ecog model.
- **coxph_concordance:** Harrell's concordance agrees with R only to ~1e-4, NOT to round-off. This is a definitional difference, not a defect: pystatistics computes the classic Harrell C from comparable pairs, while R's survival >= 3.x reports a tie-aware concordance (Therneau's concordancefit, which counts time ties and is computed via an O(n log n) balanced-tree algorithm). The two definitions differ at the fourth decimal on data with tied times; both are valid C-statistics.
- **ties_efron_vs_breslow:** Efron (the default, matching R) and Breslow are both implemented for tied event times. The validation pins ties='efron' on both sides; Efron is the more accurate approximation and R's default. Breslow agreement is not separately tabulated here.
- **discrete_vs_r:** Discrete-time covariate coefficients, standard errors, Wald z-statistics, and p-values match R glm(binomial) on the identical person-period design to fp64 round-off (~1e-14). This holds both on the small lung design (coarse quantile-binned intervals; see limitations) and on the large real flchain person-period design (yearly bins, ~83k rows): coefficients ~1e-13, standard errors ~1e-15, z ~1e-13, p ~1e-12 — fp64 agreement at genuine scale.
- **discrete_gpu_fp64:** On CUDA, discrete_time(backend='gpu_fp64') is the exact double-precision GPU path: covariate coefficients match the CPU fp64 fit to ~1e-15 (numerically exact) across the bin-granularity sweep, including the large monthly design (~1M person-period rows). This is the robust large-scale GPU path.
- **discrete_gpu_fp32:** The float32 GPU path (backend='gpu') is judged at the float32 tier by the relative Newton decrement (RIGOR R9: never against R's absolute fp64 criterion). On CUDA the float32 fit converges across the ENTIRE flchain person-period sweep (yearly through monthly, ~1M rows), covariate coefficients matching the CPU fp64 fit to ~1e-5. On Apple Silicon (MPS, no float64) the float32 GLM inner solve is, as of 4.2.4, a gated matrix-free conjugate gradient on H v = Xt(W(X v)) (squaring-free -- XtWX is never formed; the host float64 Newton-decrement acceptance gate is unchanged). With it MPS now converges across the whole flchain sweep -- including quarterly and monthly, which failed loud (not-positive-definite float32 Cholesky) at 4.2.1/4.2.3 -- covariate coefficients ~1e-5 vs CPU fp64. On a genuinely float32-infeasible design the gate still refuses loud, recommending backend='cpu' (Apple Silicon has no float64, so there is no gpu_fp64 path on MPS). Torch-version caveat: the MPS solver convergence envelope is validated against the shipped torch (2.12.1 here) and is torch-version-sensitive; the host float64 acceptance gate is the version-independent guarantee that a wrong call fails loud rather than returning silently wrong. The robust large-scale GPU path remains CUDA: float32 across the whole sweep, or gpu_fp64 for an exact (~1e-13) fit.
- **discrete_default_r15:** RIGOR R15 (validate the DEFAULT invocation, not just the expert case). The call a naive user makes -- discrete_time(intervals=None) on continuous data, which makes one interval per unique event time -- is validated as a behaviour-match against R glm(binomial) on the IDENTICAL every-unique-event-time person-period design (the design is reconstructed and guarded by the person_period_n check). Across survival::lung and continuous synthetic designs (censored seeds and a no-censoring separation extreme), the default returns a fit whose covariate coefficients match R to fp64 round-off (~1e-15) and whose deviance/AIC match R to ~1e-15 -- with R itself converging in 7-12 iterations and emitting NO warnings. Even in the separation-prone extreme (no censoring; a sparse tail interval's baseline hazard reaches 1.0 and its dummy coefficient is large but finite ~13.7), pystatistics reproduces R's behaviour and coefficients exactly. The default does not fail loud, does not warn, and does NOT return silent garbage: it agrees with the reference, so the covariate (hazard-ratio) inference is sound. The default is correct (priority-1 correctness), correcting the earlier limitation note that presumed it degenerate.
- **discrete_fp32_boundary_r13:** RIGOR R13 (a forwarded/inherited guarantee must be re-proven on the new regime) + R12 (the fp32 no-silent-wrong proof). discrete_time forwards backend= to the regression binomial GLM, so the regression R12 fp32 gate is INHERITED -- but not for free: discrete-time's person-period regime (heavy low-weight interval-dummy blocks from low baseline hazard, sparse near-separating intervals) is adversarial in ways the well-behaved flchain sweep never stressed. The R12 boundary is therefore re-run on discrete-time's OWN regime -- synthetic person-period designs stressed along four axes (low_hazard heavy-low-weight block, near_separation, large_coef, conditioning) plus a real flchain-quarterly anchor -- on BOTH MPS (gated-CG) and CUDA (Cholesky). Every ACCEPTED float32 fit reached the float64 optimum (deviance to the float32 tier, <= ~1e-3; observed ~1e-7..1e-13) and every refusal is fail-loud, dominated by true positives / conservative refusals that force=True recovers correctly (the genuinely ill-conditioned cond >= 2e5 designs). NO design was accepted-but-wrong: there is no silent-wrong band on either device. Per R9/G3, designs whose fp64 reference is itself degenerate (a uniformly deep baseline yields zero events, so no optimum exists) are EXCLUDED by a reference-degeneracy guard rather than judged -- you cannot prove 'no silent-wrong fp32' against an undefined fp64 reference; this guard caught a generator artifact during the red-team, not a library defect.
- **discrete_inference_se_r13:** R12/R13 extended from the optimum to the INFERENCE outputs (the differentiator). On the same discrete-time person-period boundary sweep, for every ACCEPTED float32 GPU fit the covariate standard errors track the fp64 CPU fit to the documented Tier-2 contract (rtol <= 1e-2; observed <= ~4e-5) and no covariate SE is understated (gpu_se >= 0.5*cpu_se on every accepted fit), on both MPS and CUDA. The float32 discrete-time path never returns a covariate that is wrong AND looks precise -- instability surfaces in the SE exactly as it does in R. This is mathematically necessary (the SE is sqrt(diag of the same (XtWX)^-1 that drives any coefficient instability) and confirmed empirically in float32 on discrete-time's regime.
- **discrete_r8_crossmodule:** RIGOR R8 cross-module check. discrete_time forwards to the binomial GLM, and 4.3.x shipped GLM-level changes (the 4.3.2 binomial deviance/AIC/BIC machine-epsilon clamp; the 4.3.0->4.3.2 GPU-OLS-SE fix). Tested, not assumed: discrete_time covariate coef/SE/z/p are BIT-IDENTICAL between v4.2.4 and v4.3.2 on the lung coarse-bin design (drift max_abs = 0, max_rel = 0), and the CPU-vs-R agreement is unchanged (~1e-15). The GPU-OLS-SE fix is OLS-specific (discrete-time uses the binomial GLM path) and does not touch it. The binomial deviance clamp COULD in principle move glm_deviance/glm_aic where fitted probabilities reach ~1; the R15 default study confirms it does not introduce a discrepancy -- discrete_time's deviance and AIC match R to ~1e-15 even on the separation-prone designs where fitted probabilities hit 1.0. At 4.3.3 (additive convergence diagnostics, IRLS untouched) all discrete_time numbers remain bit-identical (drift 0), re-verified by the fresh 4.3.3 CPU correctness run.
- **discrete_convergence_c5:** 4.3.3 closes finding C5: DiscreteTimeSolution now exposes .converged (bool) and .n_iter (int), matching CoxSolution / GLMSolution (Amendment A3). A convergence flag is only useful if it is honest, so it is validated against R glm(binomial)'s OWN convergence determination on the identical person-period design (RIGOR R9/G3: a convergence claim is tested against the reference, not assumed). Across six cases -- well-posed (lung coarse 5-bin; flchain yearly), the intervals=None default, the no-censoring separation extreme, a perfectly-separated covariate, and an all-censored degenerate design -- pystatistics' .converged AND .n_iter match R bit-for-bit: well-posed designs report converged=True at the same iteration count as R (4/8/7/12); the perfectly-separated covariate reports converged=True at 18 iterations exactly as R does (R also 'converges' on separated data, stopping when the deviance plateaus at a large finite coefficient -- so converged=True is correct, not a false positive); and the all-censored degenerate design reports converged=False at the 25-iteration cap, matching R's own non-convergence (R additionally emits the fitted-probabilities-0/1 warning). No false-positive convergence claim was found. The changelog's illustrative 'all-censored -> n_iter=0' / 'separated -> converged=False' examples describe narrower code paths than these constructions exercise; the flag's actual behaviour tracks the R reference.

## Constitutional compliance & GPU backend reconciliation

This validation tracks the survival module against the PyStatistics Constitution (CONVENTIONS.md). The 4.0.0 audit surfaced six findings (C1-C3 API-shape, O1-O2 Cox performance, and a GPU convergence issue uncovered while probing discrete-time on GPU); 4.2.1 closes the last conf_int gap (C4) and adds direct GPU validation of the discrete-time path on real data; 4.2.2-4.2.4 reshape the discrete-time CPU/GPU performance and reliability (O3, G3, G4). The 4.3.2 TARGETED RED-TEAM added three findings: R15 (the intervals=None default is validated CORRECT, not the presumed footgun), R13 (the fp32 no-silent-wrong gate re-proven on discrete-time's OWN person-period regime on both MPS and CUDA), and C5 (DiscreteTimeSolution did not surface .converged/.n_iter -- a transparency gap). 4.3.3 RESOLVES C5 (adds .converged/.n_iter) and a targeted follow-up validates the new flag against R's convergence behaviour (it matches bit-for-bit, including n_iter). The classical correctness numbers are unaffected by any of these changes (R8: discrete_time is bit-identical 4.2.4->4.3.2->4.3.3); the status of each finding is recorded below.

**GPU backend reconciliation.** The constitution lists survival.coxph as a deliberate NO-GPU module (the partial likelihood is inherently sequential). The public API honors this: kaplan_meier, survdiff, and coxph expose no backend= parameter; KM and log-rank are cheap one-pass reductions and Cox PH is O(n log n) on the CPU (4.2.0). The one GPU-capable path is discrete_time, which forwards backend= to pystatistics.regression.fit(family='binomial') over the person-period expansion. Two releases since the 4.0.0 audit reshaped its performance and reliability, both validated here directly on the real survival::flchain person-period design. (1) 4.2.2 rewrote the CPU least-squares core (qr_solve) from full column-pivoted QR to a single-pass rank-revealing QR equivalent to R's lm.fit/glm.fit, so the CPU discrete-time fit now MEETS OR BEATS R at scale (monthly ~1M-row design: 58s vs R glm 71s) with correctness unchanged (~1e-15). (2) 4.2.3 fixed a float32 GPU premature-stop FALSE NEGATIVE (see finding G3): the float32 IRLS loop stopped at the float32 round-off floor while slow low-hazard interval dummies were still moving, so the strict Newton-decrement gate spuriously rejected float32-reachable fits. Replacing the stop with a decrement-based criterion (plus descent step-halving), with the strict acceptance gate untouched, makes the float32 path iterate to genuine stationarity. After both fixes: CUDA float32 converges across the entire flchain sweep including monthly (~1e-5 vs CPU), CUDA gpu_fp64 is exact (~1e-13) -- the robust large-scale GPU path, ~9.5x faster than the (now faster) CPU at monthly and ~18x in float32. Apple Silicon (MPS) went further at 4.2.4: the float32 inner solve became a gated matrix-free conjugate gradient (squaring-free), so MPS now converges across the whole flchain sweep including quarterly and monthly -- which failed loud with a not-positive-definite float32 Cholesky at 4.2.1/4.2.3 -- at the float32 tier (~1e-5 vs CPU); a genuinely float32-infeasible design still refuses loud, recommending backend='cpu' (no gpu_fp64 on Apple Silicon). The MPS convergence envelope is validated on the shipped torch (2.12.1) and is version-sensitive; the host float64 acceptance gate is the version-independent fail-loud guarantee. CUDA (Cholesky) and CPU are unchanged. See finding G4. The A6 contract holds throughout: an explicit request is honored exactly or fails loud -- never a silent CPU/precision fallback, and (since 4.2.3) never a false refusal of a float32-reachable fit. To our knowledge no prior implementation has put discrete-time survival on a GPU at this scale.

- **C1 — Coefficient solutions lacked the uniform .conf_int accessor** _(RESOLVED in 4.1.0)_: 4.1.0 added a library-wide .conf_int (Wald) + conf_level= across the coefficient models, including CoxSolution; it matches R's summary.coxph hazard-ratio intervals to ~1e-15 and removed the hardcoded 1.96 from the Cox summary. (DiscreteTimeSolution still lacks .conf_int — a minor remaining gap, tracked separately.)
- **C2 — KMSolution used .se / .ci_lower / .ci_upper rather than the uniform names** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 added KMSolution.standard_errors (Greenwood SE) and KMSolution.conf_int (the (m,2) [lower,upper] band), matching the library-wide accessor names. The legacy .se/.ci_lower/.ci_upper remain (additive).
- **C3 — Stratified KM/Cox raised the builtin NotImplementedError** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 added NotImplementedFeatureError (subclasses both PyStatisticsError and the builtin NotImplementedError); stratified KM/Cox now raise it, routing every survival error through core.exceptions while staying catchable as NotImplementedError.
- **O1 — Harrell's concordance was an O(n^2) nested Python loop** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 replaced the all-pairs double loop with an O(n log n) Fenwick-tree count, reproducing the existing Harrell definition exactly. See the scaling study: the pystatistics/R Cox wall ratio went from growing 23x->812x to flat ~1-2.6x.
- **O2 — Cox partial-likelihood/score/information recomputed risk sets per event time** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: 4.2.0 replaced the per-event-time risk-mask recomputation with vectorized reverse-cumulative-sum risk sets (S0/S1/S2) indexed via searchsorted — one O(n) sweep per Newton iteration. Together with O1 this makes Cox PH O(n log n), matching R; a full fit at n=8000 dropped from ~10s to ~31ms. Results bit-for-bit unchanged.
- **G1 — GPU GLM falsely rejected correct float32 fits (discrete-time on GPU)** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.0)_: Probing discrete_time on GPU exposed a GPU GLM convergence gate that held the strict float64 tolerance on the float32 path: a correct, well-conditioned float32 fit that reached the float32 floor was declared non-converged and failed loud (most often on MPS). The fit was correct (matched CPU to the float32 tier). 4.2.0 decides float32 acceptance by the relative Newton decrement (a stationarity test) instead; genuinely-unreliable fits still fail loud, with no silent CPU/precision fallback. Verified on MPS and CUDA across binomial/Poisson/Gamma.
- **C4 — DiscreteTimeSolution lacked the uniform .conf_int accessor** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.1)_: The 4.1.0 conf_int rollout (C1) left DiscreteTimeSolution as the one coefficient model without .conf_int (tracked as a minor remaining gap). 4.2.1 adds DiscreteTimeSolution.conf_int + .conf_level and discrete_time(conf_level=); the Wald .conf_int surface is now uniform across every coefficient model in the library. Additive, backward-compatible.
- **G2 — Discrete-time GPU path validated directly on real data** _(VALIDATED (4.2.1); mechanism corrected in 4.2.3)_: The discrete-time GPU path is validated directly on the real survival::flchain person-period design (not only inherited from the regression GPU backend). The float32 path initially appeared to fail at moderate scale; that was first attributed to noisy float32 reductions, then to initialization -- both WRONG. The correct diagnosis (a premature stop) and its fix are recorded in G3. CUDA gpu_fp64 is exact at every granularity.
- **O3 — CPU least-squares core rewritten to a single-pass rank-revealing QR (R-parity)** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.2)_: The CPU OLS/GLM solver (core qr_solve) used full column-pivoted QR (LAPACK dgeqp3) on every solve -- heavier than R's lighter rank-revealing QR (LINPACK dqrls), so CPU fits were slower than R for no benefit. 4.2.2 replaced it with a single-pass rank-revealing QR equivalent to R's, reaching speed parity-or-better with R on full-rank AND rank-deficient designs (CONVENTIONS Prime Directive: CPU parity with R is a requirement). For survival this makes the GLM-backed discrete-time CPU fit meet/beat R at scale (monthly 58s vs R 71s); coefficients unchanged (~1e-15). Cox PH is unaffected (it uses a Newton solve on the information matrix, not qr_solve).
- **G3 — float32 GLM IRLS spuriously failed to converge (premature-stop false negative)** _(RESOLVED in 4.2.3)_: On float32 GPU paths the binomial IRLS loop stopped at the float32 round-off floor (eff_tol ~ 2*sqrt(n)*eps_fp32) at ~iteration 5, while slowly-converging low-baseline-hazard interval dummies were still moving ~1 logit/iteration; the strict Newton-decrement acceptance gate then correctly saw a non-stationary iterate and FAILED LOUD -- a false negative, since float32 can reach the fit (the Newton decrement falls to ~1e-12 if the loop continues). Earlier guesses (noisy reductions; initialization) were ruled out by measurement: MPS forms XtWX to ~3e-6, the solve to ~4e-6, and a step from the optimum to ~2e-5 -- the arithmetic is sound; the STOP CRITERION was the defect. 4.2.3 replaces the float32 stop with the relative Newton decrement (the same measure the gate uses) plus monotone-descent step-halving; the strict fp64 acceptance gate is unchanged (no new silently-accepted fits). CUDA float32 now converges across the full flchain sweep including monthly; MPS float32 converges on most grids but stays intermittently fragile (genuine float32 Cholesky breakdowns at some sizes still fail loud, A6-correct). The genuinely float32-infeasible regime (where cond*eps_fp32 exceeds the gate) still fails loud on every backend -- gpu_fp64/CPU are the answer there.
- **G4 — MPS float32 GLM inner solve moved to a gated matrix-free conjugate gradient** _(VALIDATED in 4.2.4)_: Through 4.2.3 the float32 GLM inner solve formed XtWX and factorized it by Cholesky. On Apple Silicon (MPS) that squares the condition number, and at the flchain quarterly person-period grid the float32 XtWX was not positive-definite from a cold MPS context, so the inner Cholesky broke down and the fit failed loud (A6-correct, but a usable design refused). 4.2.4 replaces the MPS float32 inner solve with a gated matrix-free conjugate gradient on the operator H v = Xt(W(X v)) -- XtWX is never formed (squaring-free) -- while leaving the host float64 Newton-decrement acceptance gate untouched. Validated here on the real survival::flchain sweep: MPS now converges at every granularity including quarterly (coef ~4e-5 vs CPU) and monthly (~9e-6), where 4.2.1/4.2.3 were blank/rejected. On a genuinely float32-infeasible design the gate still refuses loud, now recommending backend='cpu' (Apple Silicon has no float64, so no gpu_fp64). CUDA keeps its Cholesky path (robust; gpu_fp64 exact ~1e-13) and CPU is unchanged -- both match 4.2.3 exactly. Caveat: MPS kernel behaviour is torch-version-sensitive, so the convergence envelope is validated per torch version (2.12.1 here); the host float64 gate is the version-independent guarantee that a wrong call stays loud.
- **R15 — The default discrete_time(intervals=None) is validated correct, not a footgun** _(VALIDATED in 4.3.2)_: The 4.3.2 red-team closed RIGOR R15 (validate the DEFAULT invocation). The frozen validation had only covered coarse expert-chosen bins and recorded the intervals=None default as presumptively degenerate ('separates perfectly; neither engine converges cleanly'). Tested empirically, that premise did not hold: on continuous data the default makes one interval per unique event time, and pystatistics matches R glm(binomial) on the identical every-unique-event-time person-period design to fp64 round-off (covariate coefficients ~1e-15; deviance/AIC ~1e-15), with R converging in 7-12 iterations and emitting no warnings -- including the no-censoring separation extreme where a sparse tail interval's baseline hazard reaches 1.0 (its dummy coefficient large but finite ~13.7, exactly as R). The default does not fail loud, does not warn, and does not return silent garbage; the covariate (hazard-ratio) inference is sound. The earlier limitation note is corrected accordingly.
- **R13 — fp32 no-silent-wrong gate re-proven on discrete-time's OWN person-period regime** _(VALIDATED in 4.3.2)_: RIGOR R13: a guarantee inherited via forwarding (discrete_time -> regression binomial GLM) must be re-proven on the new regime. The novelty path ('first discrete-time survival on GPU at scale') thus carries its own re-proven no-silent-wrong evidence, not an inherited one; a prior-art trawl (arXiv/PyPI/GitHub) remains owed before any publication.
- **C5 — DiscreteTimeSolution does not surface .converged / .n_iter** _(RESOLVED in 4.3.3 (was OPEN at 4.3.2))_: Surfaced by the 4.3.2 red-team: at 4.3.2 the public DiscreteTimeSolution exposed neither a convergence flag nor an iteration count, unlike CoxSolution and the GLMSolution it delegates to (both carry .converged / .n_iter per Amendment A3) -- a transparency gap (not a correctness defect; the R15 default study confirms the underlying fit is correct). 4.3.3 adds .converged (bool) and .n_iter (int) to DiscreteTimeSolution plus a summary() 'Converged: ...' line (additive, backward-compatible). ## 6. What are the known limitations?

- Cox PH is CPU-only by construction — the partial likelihood is inherently sequential and is not a GPU target (a constitutional decision, not a gap). As of 4.2.0 the CPU implementation is O(n log n) (vectorized reverse-cumsum risk sets + a Fenwick-tree concordance), matching R's complexity; the scaling study shows a flat pystatistics/R wall ratio. True O(n) is impossible — the event-time sort is O(n log n), and R is O(n log n) too.
- Harrell's concordance differs from R's tie-aware concordance at ~1e-4 (a definitional difference; see tolerances). Both are valid C-statistics.
- Stratified Kaplan-Meier and stratified Cox PH are not implemented (strata=... raises NotImplementedFeatureError). This is correct fail-loud behaviour (A6), but stratified Cox is real, commonly-needed functionality for biostatisticians (the survival analog of the weights/offset support added to regression at 4.3.0) — it is recorded here as a known missing feature, not merely an edge case. The validation covers the unstratified models that R's lung examples use.
- The public DiscreteTimeSolution does not surface a convergence flag or iteration count: unlike CoxSolution and the GLMSolution it delegates to (both expose .converged / .n_iter), a discrete_time user at 4.3.2 cannot tell whether the person-period IRLS converged or hit the iteration cap. This is a transparency gap (not a correctness defect — the underlying fit is correct, see the R15 default study) surfaced by this red-team; a fix that propagates .converged / .n_iter onto DiscreteTimeSolution is staged for the v4.3.3 library cycle.
- Discrete-time survival is validated BOTH on coarse expert-chosen bins (5-bin lung; calendar-binned flchain) AND on the intervals=None DEFAULT (RIGOR R15). The earlier concern that the default 'separates perfectly and neither engine converges cleanly' did not survive empirical test: on continuous data the default makes one interval per unique event time, and pystatistics matches R glm(binomial) on the identical every-unique-event-time person-period design to fp64 round-off (~1e-15 on covariate coefficients), with R itself converging in 7-12 iterations and emitting no warnings — including the separation-prone extreme (no censoring) where a sparse tail interval's baseline hazard reaches 1.0 and its dummy coefficient is large but finite, exactly as R produces. The covariate inference (the user-facing hazard ratios) is sound throughout; the default is correct, not a footgun. Coarse binning remains the statistically preferred choice for genuinely binned time, but it is a modelling preference, not a correctness requirement.
- discrete_time is the one GPU-capable survival path (it forwards backend= to the regression binomial GLM over the person-period expansion), validated directly on real survival::flchain. The robust GPU path is CUDA: backend='gpu_fp64' is exact (~1e-13 vs CPU) at every granularity, and backend='gpu' (float32) converges across the whole flchain sweep including monthly (~1e-5). On Apple Silicon (MPS, no float64) the 4.2.4 float32 inner solve is a gated matrix-free conjugate gradient (squaring-free), and MPS now converges across the whole flchain sweep including quarterly and monthly -- the grids that failed loud (not-positive-definite float32 Cholesky) at 4.2.1/4.2.3. Genuinely float32-infeasible designs (cond*eps_fp32 above the acceptance gate) still fail loud on every backend, with MPS recommending backend='cpu' (no gpu_fp64 on Apple Silicon). The MPS convergence envelope is validated on the shipped torch (2.12.1) and is version-sensitive; the host float64 acceptance gate is the version-independent fail-loud guarantee.
- The person-period expansion (pystatistics/survival/_discrete.py) is a pure-Python double loop, but it is a small fraction of end-to-end discrete_time at scale (~1.7% at the monthly ~1M-row design); the binomial GLM fit dominates, which is why the GPU speedup is reported on the fit. As of 4.2.2 the CPU fit itself meets/beats R at scale (monthly 58s vs R glm 71s); the GPU (CUDA) is faster still (~4s gpu_fp64).
- Only the Efron tie handling (R's default) is tabulated in the head-to-head; Breslow is implemented but not separately validated here.
- Tiny real datasets (lung, n~227) sit near R's timer resolution for KM and log-rank; the synthetic scaling study, not the lung timings, carries the rigorous performance claim.
